1. Summary

We label every frame of the tactical video as H, A or D. We never write X on graded frames. Every frame gets two probabilities from the tracking data: p_dead, the chance that play is stopped, and p_home, the chance that the home team has the ball if play is live. A three-state Viterbi decoder then picks the single most likely H/A/D sequence for each half. It charges a fixed cost for every change of state, which removes the frame-to-frame flicker that ruins per-frame voting under the spell-based metric.

All learned parts are small logistic regressions written in numpy. Each is fitted on Metrica's three public, labelled sample games, never on the provided match. Every constant was chosen on those games with held-out (leave-one-game-out) scoring. The public 5-minute ground truth was used only as a final correctness check.

2. Method

2.1 Data layer (Section 1)

  • The ATD (FIFA/EPTS) is parsed into per-frame arrays: video_frame = frame_id − 1, positions in metres on the 105.3 × 68.0 m pitch from games.py, and missing observations stored as NaN, never as a sentinel value. The 46 track slots carry a team but no identity, so every feature is a team-level statistic and none needs player identity.
  • About 9.8% of tracked speeds are NaN, so presence is tested on positions, and all aggregates are nan-aware.

2.2 Ball (Section 2)

  • The ball is missing on 41% of graded frames, mostly in long gaps: gaps over 10 s hold about 85% of the missing frames. Spikes and points more than 8 m outside the pitch are removed. Gaps inside a half are filled with a straight line, and nothing is carried across half-time.
  • ball_conf is 1 where the ball is observed. In a gap it falls with the distance to the nearest observation and with the gap length, down to a floor of 0.05. In a seeded test that hid 1,045 observed stretches and refilled them, the median fill error was 0.02 m for gaps of 1–5 frames, 1.1 m for 26–75 frames and 4.5 m for 76–250 frames. Its rank correlation with ball_conf was −0.84, so the confidence orders the fills correctly.

2.3 Dead ball (Section 3)

A logistic regression gives p_dead from two kinds of evidence:

  • Collective movement. The median and 90th-percentile player speed, averaged over windows of 0.5–10 s, plus 3 s windows 2 s ahead and 2 s behind. When play stops, everybody walks. The speed distributions of our match and of the sample games are almost the same, which is why the model transfers.
  • Ball over a line. An observed (not interpolated) ball beyond a touchline or goal line within the last 3 s.

A missing ball is never treated as evidence of a dead ball. On our match the ball is still tracked on about two thirds of dead frames, and most missing-ball frames are in open play.

2.4 Team in possession (Section 4a)

Two logistic regressions each produce a logit for "home has the ball". They are blended by how sure we are of the ball position:

logit = ball_conf · ball_logit + (1 − ball_conf) · shape_logit     p_home = σ(logit)
  • Ball evidence (fitted on sample-game live frames with an observed ball). It uses the signed gap between the nearest-home and nearest-away distances to the ball, capped at 20 m. That gap is also averaged over 0.5, 2 and 5 s, together with its ±0.5 s trend, the share of the last 2 s that home was nearer, a "someone is on the ball" term that fades out at 3 m, and the nearest player's distance.
  • Team-shape evidence (fitted on every sample-game live frame, and needs no ball). The team in possession stretches out, while the defending team stays compact. Features are each team's spread along and across the pitch, the home-minus-away difference, and the same averaged over 1 s and 4 s and over 3 s before and after the frame. A frame where either team has fewer than 7 tracked players gets no shape evidence.

Shape evidence matters because gaps average 17 s and often contain several hand-overs. On held-out sample games, ball evidence alone names the right team on about 90% of observed-ball frames but only 52–63% of gap frames. Shape evidence names it on 83–84% of gap frames.

2.5 Decoder (Section 4b)

Each half is decoded on its own with a three-state Viterbi over {H, A, D}. With q = σ(1.5 · logit(p_home)), the per-frame cost of each state is −log of its score:

score(H) = (1 − p_dead) · q    score(A) = (1 − p_dead) · (1 − q)    score(D) = p_dead

A change between H and A costs 10, and entering or leaving D costs 20. These three constants came from a grid search on the sample games (leave-one-game-out). They sit on a flat optimum: most settings with team weight 1.0–2.0, H↔A cost 10–25 and D cost 10–40 score within 0.005 spellF1 of the best. Frames outside the graded halves are written as X.

3. Possession rules implemented

The ground truth defines possession mechanically from corrected tracking. Our implementation follows the same rules through the decoder's costs:

SituationWhat our pipeline does
Touches, deflections, won aerial duels without controlNo change. A brief dip in the evidence cannot pay for two switches. The evidence has to point the other way strongly for about 10 frames or more before the label flips.
Loose or contested ballStays with the last possessor. A ball far from everyone gives p_home near 0.5, which never pays a switch cost.
Hand-overNeeds sustained evidence: the proximity margin or team shape has to favour the other side for about a second or more.
Ball not trackedBall evidence fades with ball_conf and team shape takes over. A sustained change of shape hands the ball over inside the gap. With too few players tracked, the last possessor keeps it.
Stoppage (D)Sustained high p_dead moves the path to D, which ends the possession. A brief false alarm does not.
RestartAfter D, the next team is whichever the evidence favours. The decoder has no memory of who had the ball before the stoppage.
Half-timeHalves are decoded separately, and frames 110808–113741 before the 2nd-half kick-off come out as D from p_dead.
Abstention (X)Never written on graded frames. The decoder already holds the last possessor through weak evidence, which is all X would gain inside a spell, and X across a hand-over is penalised.

6. Limitations

  • Proximity is not control. On observed-ball frames, p_home mostly measures who is nearest and for how long. A defender beside a passing attacker counts as the nearer team for a moment, and a long pass reads as a loose ball until a receiver is close.
  • Team shape is a tendency, not a rule. A high press or a counter-attack with few players can look like the other side's shape for a few seconds. On sample-game gap frames it is right about 84% of the time, and probably less on our match.
  • Track dropouts. On 7% of graded frames a team has fewer than 7 tracked players, so those frames get no shape evidence. The shape model was fitted on clean sample tracks.
  • Restarts with movement. Quick throw-ins and goal kicks, where players keep jogging, are the stoppages missed most often. A missed stoppage between two spells of the same team merges them.
  • Proxy labels. Sample-game labels come from event data, while the target comes from corrected tracking, so stoppage edges and hand-over times are defined slightly differently.
  • First-order decoder. It has no notion of which team usually restarts after a given stoppage, or of how long possessions tend to last.
  • Smart Tagging unused. Its restart-team hints are likely useful, but we had no labelled source to calibrate them on.

Next steps, by expected value: separate control from proximity using ball speed and direction changes. Degrade the sample tracks like ours before fitting the shape model. Use Smart Tagging restart teams as priors once they can be calibrated. Make switch costs restart-aware. Try X inside long low-confidence stretches.
