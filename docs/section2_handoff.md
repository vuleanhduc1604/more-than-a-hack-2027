# Section 2 handoff (ball-v0, tagging-v1)

From the repo root, with the data in `data/` or `MTAH_DATA_DIR`. First check that `wc -l data/tactical/BAR-ATH_tactical_atd.txt` gives 199319, because a cut-off download makes the loader stop with "expected 3 fields".

```python
import sys; sys.path.insert(0, "starter")
import ball, tagging
b = ball.load()      # ball_xy (199319, 2) float32, ball_conf (199319,) float32, ball_method (199319,) uint8
t = tagging.load()   # restart (199319,) float32, st_team (199319,) int8, restart_team (199319,) int8
```

Row i is frame i of the tactical video. Both calls cache to `cache/` and rebuild when the data, `VERSION` or the parameters change. Check `ball.VERSION` and `tagging.VERSION` if you cache anything derived from these arrays.

- `ball_xy`: ball position in metres, with no NaN on scored frames. Gaps inside a half are straight lines, and the ends of a half hold the nearest observation. It is never carried across halftime, and frames outside the scored blocks stay NaN where the tracker had no ball. Use `ball.scored_blocks()` to get the scored blocks as half-open ranges.
- **Most of the missing ball sits in long gaps.** Gaps over 10 s hold about 85% of the missing frames, and gaps over 3 s about 99%. Inside those gaps the straight line is close to a guess, so `ball_conf` is at its floor. Read those frames as "position unknown". Some long gaps may be stoppages, which is your call in Section 3.
- `ball_xy` is **not clipped to the pitch**. Points up to 8 m outside the lines are kept, so off-pitch detection works. Spikes, and points further out than 8 m, are removed and then filled. Points further out are fairly common, so v1 may expose them as a separate signal.
- `ball_conf`: 1.0 where the ball was observed. In a gap it falls with the distance to the nearest observation and with the gap length, with a floor of 0.05. It is 0.0 outside the scored blocks where there is no ball. It is only roughly calibrated until v1, so read it as an ordering rather than as a probability.
- **Fill error (plan step 9), from `python starter/ball.py --fill-error`.** It hides 1,045 stretches of observed ball (seeded), refills them with the same code, and measures the miss in metres. Median error by gap length is 0.02 m (1–5 frames), 0.06 (6–12), 0.26 (13–25), 1.1 (26–75), 4.5 (76–250) and 7.8 (>250, only 2 gaps). A straight line beats holding the last point at every length. By `ball_conf`, the median error falls from 7.7 m (conf < 0.1) to 0.01 m (conf > 0.9), and the rank correlation is −0.84, so `ball_conf` is a sound ordering. These numbers are a lower bound, because the hidden stretches sit in open play where the tracker could see the ball. Real gaps (85% are over 10 s) are mostly beyond what this test can sample.
- `ball_method`: 0 observed, 1 linear, 2 hold, 3 player-based (last resort only), 255 outside the scored blocks.
- `restart`: in v0, 1.0 over each set-piece tag window and 0 elsewhere. **The windows are long, about 29 to 35 s**, so `restart` is 1 on about 28% of scored frames. Read it as "a set piece happens somewhere in this half-minute", not as the restart moment. In v1 it becomes a soft bump that peaks near the likely restart.
- `st_team` (tagging-v1): 0 home, 1 away, -1 unknown. It is the team the phase tags say **has the ball**, by majority vote of the tags covering a frame, and -1 on a tie. Polarity is measured, not assumed. For each code, `tagging.py` checks how often the nearest player to the observed ball belongs to the tagged team, and tests that against 0.5 across the code's tags. On this match, all four defending codes come out as naming the team without the ball (t from -2.9 to -9.4), so they are flipped. The three box codes (`ball in the box`, `players in the box`, `creating chances`) and their mirror `defending in defensive third` show no clear sign, so they are dropped. As a result, frames where both teams get votes fell from 41.5% to 6.1% of scored frames, and `st_team` is now known on 56% of scored frames (home 35.9%, away 20.2%). The tag windows still open early, so this is a hint about the half-minute around a frame, not a handover time.
- `restart_team` (new in tagging-v1): 0 home, 1 away, -1 unknown, over each set-piece window. It is the team taking the restart. Corners, free kicks and goal kicks keep their tagged team. As a check, on all 6 corners with a clear player at the flag, that player's team matches the tag. Throw-ins (all tagged n/a) are inferred from tracking as the team with a player 0.3 to 3 m beyond the touchline, with an extra vote when that player is near an observed ball. 17 of 26 throw-ins are decided (7 home, 10 away), In a prototype check, a rule using only player positions and a rule needing a seen ball were run separately, and they agreed on every throw-in both decided (10 to 12, depending on thresholds). The other 9 throw-ins and all 6 kick-offs stay -1. The window covers the whole 30-odd seconds around the set piece, so the restart team is not the possessing team for all of it.
- All five arrays are evidence, not labels. `st_team` and `restart_team` are hints from automatic tags, and they are wrong some of the time.
